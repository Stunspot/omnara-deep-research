## One real inquiry, not a template score

The question was whether a falling local tide-gauge trend contradicts rising global mean sea level. Two searches and direct official-page reading supplied NOAA relative/global definitions, NASA global indicator scope and a legacy NOAA FAQ. The answer correctly distinguishes reference frame and aggregation before comparing trend signs. It does not infer that either dataset is erroneous, diagnose a station, extract an unobserved numeric rate or call a future local flood threshold established. The legacy FAQ's historical numerical comparison was rejected while preserving the fact it was read. NOAA pages share institutional lineage and are not independent empirical replications.

The meaningful next task is to obtain the actual series, common period/units, reference definitions, land-motion information and processing notes. Reading another general sea-level explanation would not close that gap. A future design request changes the evidence burden to site/scenario/planning horizon; it cannot inherit completion merely because the topic is still sea level. These distinctions are explicit in report, notes, claim treatments, practice and return guidance. No raw data analysis or independent research peer review occurred.
