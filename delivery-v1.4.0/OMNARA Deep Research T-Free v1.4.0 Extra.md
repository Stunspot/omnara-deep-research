# Description

OMNARA helps you investigate a substantial question and turn what you learn into a source-grounded answer. Break the inquiry into useful questions, read real material beside your notes, connect passages to findings, compare explanations and write the answer with its uncertainty visible. The local research room supports this work directly; the skill brings the broader research craft to your AI host.

# Usage Notes

Copy the .zip from "Additional Files" to your Codex, Claude Code, or other harness, or add the .zip to a project knowledge base in Chat. Attach or reference the .zip in chat and say, "Install this Augment." The prompt in this post is optional.

Extract the complete kit and open Open.cmd on Windows or Open.command on macOS with Python 3.10 or later. Start an investigation with a question and why the answer matters. Add documents or images, browse an existing source folder, or paste a passage with its source. A link alone remains a lead; search links open the real browser rather than claiming that the app has read a page.

Select a passage and use **Make a finding from selected text**. Explain what follows and what could challenge it, then use the finding in your answer. Edit the actual answer, add the source list and copy or export readable work. Existing campaigns appear when their work folder is selected; **Work folder** chooses a writable location without command-line setup, and **Save changes** preserves the current work. Detailed existing research views remain available under **More tools**.

Original files are retained in the work folder's `.materials` directory; source locations are untouched. Text PDFs and Word documents are read locally, while scanned PDFs need OCR for searchable text. Files are limited to 32 MB. Back up the whole work folder when moving an inquiry, and copy unsaved text separately before reloading after a conflict. The app does not fabricate findings or certify a claim merely because it has a source attached.

Windows ordinary-input, material and storage workflows were exercised. Native macOS launch remains untested in this pass.
